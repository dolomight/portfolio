Overview

A clinical-trial platform built for aesthetics medicine, not borrowed from pharma.

Allergan Aesthetics, an AbbVie company, runs clinical studies where the evidence is a face: before-and-after images and video, captured consistently, across many sites and many weeks. The off-the-shelf trial software on the market was built for molecule and compound trials. It was decentralised, time-consuming to manage, frustrating for sites and subjects, and it slowed everything down.

The Clinical Innovation team started with a question: could a subject's own phone capture study images at the same quality as the clinic-grade imaging systems studies relied on? The proof of concept answered yes, at 94% parity, and that answer became a product. Aptios grew into one platform with modules to recruit, train, capture, analyse and administer studies, used by subjects, investigators, clinicians, research coordinators, sponsors, study teams and data monitors.

With Fahlgren Mortine I have led the work since 2021 as an extension of AbbVie's product team: naming and building the Aptios brand from scratch, architecting the app and the admin console, defining the road map and every feature that followed, and running UX, UI, brand design and QC through monthly release sprints. Along the way we added a 3D treatment visualizer, eConsent, integrations with machine-learning analysis, and micro-targeted paid media to recruit study subjects.

The brand

Aptios: a name, a mark and a system, built from a blank page

The platform needed an identity that could sit inside the Allergan Aesthetics brand and still stand on its own in an app store, a study site and a sponsor's slide deck. We ran the naming and positioning workshops that produced Aptios, "keenly intelligent and responsive", and its tagline, Empowering Results.

The ring in the mark, segmented in the Aptios oranges, became the system's signature: cropped as a design element, used as a progress cue in the app, and extended into module lockups for Recruit, Train, Capture, Analyze and the Admin Console. The brand standards cover voice, nomenclature, typography, colour, accessibility, iconography, UI components and templates, so every team touching Aptios builds the same thing.

Remote capture app

Study-grade images from a phone in someone's kitchen

The app is where the platform earns its keep. A subject's home screen is a timeline of what is due: a video capture, an image capture, a data-capture questionnaire, each with the time it will take and a single Start Session button. Clinicians use an assisted capture mode to run the same sessions in the clinic.

Capture itself is guided frame by frame. The camera finds the face, reads the lighting in each quadrant and tells the subject what is too dark or too bright before they can continue, then checks distance and alignment. During the capture a target tracks the nose through the turn so every angle the protocol needs is recorded, and the app asks for a re-take the moment the subject drifts. Consistency is what makes the images usable by the analysis algorithms downstream, so the app never lets a capture proceed until the conditions are right.

Admin console

One console for seven kinds of user, from sponsor to subject

The admin console is the platform's operating system. Sponsors and study teams configure studies, visits and capture schedules; sites manage their users and subjects; investigators and data monitors work the query and data-change workflows; everyone trains in the Train module and carries a certificate of completion. We defined and designed every one of those journeys, and we keep extending them.

eConsent is the clearest example of the platform growing to replace paper. Study teams build consent documents section by section in the console, in as many languages as the study needs, with single-choice and assessment questions inline. Subjects read and sign on their phone or on the web, get a confirmation email with their completed document, and the record is kept within HIPAA security requirements.

3D visualizer

A 3D configurator for planning treatment, from the first Unity model to a working prototype

The 3D work began as a Unity model inside the Train module, a rendered head that shows study teams exactly what each expression in a protocol looks like. It has grown into the 3D Configurator, a browser tool for planning and reviewing treatment on a model of the face or the full body. Users pick a model, switch anatomical layers on and off, choose treatment areas and injection points from a listing, and compare the model against a subject's captured photos side by side.

The configurator is in prototype now, designed in Figma and built in Unity, and it shares its engine with the algorithm work that connects captured images to machine-learning analysis.

Recruitment

Filling studies with search, paid media and influencers

A study only produces evidence if the right subjects enrol, so recruitment became part of the platform rather than a separate agency brief. We built branded landing pages with screening flows that qualify candidates on criteria such as skin type before they ever contact a site, then drove micro-targeted search, paid social and influencer campaigns into them, study by study and market by market.

Each campaign is measured through to enrolment, which lets sponsors see cost per qualified subject alongside the study's other operating data.

How we work

An extension of the team, shipping every month since the proof of concept

Aptios is a GxP-compliant product that touches subject data, so every release carries architecture review, security and penetration testing, and SDLC documentation. We run it as a programme, not a project: monthly sprints that turn the road map into releases across the admin console and the app, with the client free to reprioritise as new studies come on.

Around the product we cover what a small internal team cannot: user guides for studies, sites and subjects; training for the platform and each module; branded templates and marketing materials; and program management across AbbVie's infrastructure, review boards and integration partners.

The impact

From a proof of concept to the platform studies ask for

In three years Aptios went from a single question about phone cameras to a product in full development, and it has passed every step of AbbVie's internal review process along the way. Adoption is spreading as more studies see what a purpose-built platform does for their timelines, and the business case projects multi-million-dollar annual savings against the tools it replaces. Groups beyond R&D, in commercial operations and consumer brands, have asked for it too.

The relationship has outlasted three other vendors on the programme. That is the real outcome: a client that trusts one team to define, design, build and support its product, month after month.
